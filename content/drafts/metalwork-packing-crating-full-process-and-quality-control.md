Luxury boutiques, high-end residences and luxury hotels: the metalwork is valuable, the surfaces delicate and the profiles irregular. Packing and crating is not "wrapping up" — it is surface protection, corner protection, structural fixing and traceable labelling as one integrated system. This guide takes 12 main operations, 36 sub-operations, 6 structural details and 12 defect controls as its spine, breaking down the complete chain from cleaning and filming to loading and transport, layer by layer.

## 1. Packing Priorities Across the Three Application Scenes

One packing logic, three different protection grades. The scene decides the packaging materials, the fixing method and the transport requirements — luxury retail demands a flawless surface, premium residences lightweight easy opening, and luxury hotels batch-to-batch consistency.

### 1.1 Luxury Retail Flagship

![Luxury retail packing in the field: mirror and PVD titanium panels standing in a custom pearl-cotton slot crate, double-protected with PE film and non-woven cloth](img:01)

Retail metal finishes are valuable and delicate (mirror, PVD titanium) — any scratch or dent is irreparable. Packing centres on a zero-defect surface: vertical plywood crates with pearl-cotton lining and custom-fitted slots.

- **Mirror / PVD panels** — double protection with PE film + non-woven fabric
- **Shaped parts** — custom pearl-cotton slots, moulded in one piece
- **Transport** — vertical crate only, flat stacking strictly prohibited
- **Traceability** — QR code scanned for fabrication parameters and installation position
- **Film** — stays on until unpacking on site
- **Weight limit** — max 800 kg per crate; split if overweight

### 1.2 Premium Residence

![Premium residence packing in the field: brushed skirtings and casings wrapped in bubble wrap and pearl cotton, sorted cartons, one-person carry](img:02)

Residential metalwork is mostly skirtings, door casings and wall inlay strips — many pieces, small batches, tight site space. Packing centres on lightweight easy opening: cartons + bubble wrap + pearl cotton, without over-packaging.

- **Long-grain brushed parts** — wrapped in bubble wrap + pearl cotton
- **Cartons sorted by specification**, each carton with a packing list
- **Lightweight packing** — one-person carry (≤ 25 kg)
- **Labels** state the room number and installation position
- **Film** stays on until installation
- **Cartons recyclable**, less site waste

### 1.3 Luxury Hospitality

![Luxury hotel packing in the field: batch panels in steel frames double-padded with pearl cotton and EVA, stackable up to 3 layers](img:03)

Hotels mean large volumes, many batches and long transport distances. Packing centres on batch consistency and stackability: steel frames + pearl cotton + EVA pads, balancing protection against transport efficiency.

- **Etched / embossed parts** — double padding with pearl cotton + EVA
- **Steel-frame packing** — stackable up to 3 layers
- **Batch management** — same zone in the same crate, easy site distribution
- **Labels** state the floor + zone + installation position
- **Transport** — no rain exposure, no laying on the side in transit
- **Frames reusable**, cutting long-term packaging cost

> Key point: the three scenes share one protection logic but not one standard. Retail buys a flawless surface with a vertical crate; residence buys speed with a carton; hotel buys efficiency with a reusable frame. Matching the grade to the scene is what keeps over-packing out of the budget.

## 3. Packing & Crating · 12 Main Operations · 36 Sub-Operations

Packing is not "wrapping up" — it is a complete chain from cleaning, filming and corner guards through cushioning, fixing and labelling to loading and delivery. Every step has clear control points; a lapse at any step can escalate into irreversible damage in transit.

### 3.1 Final Inspection and Cleaning

![Final inspection and cleaning in the field: neutral detergent wipe and compressed-air dusting — packing starts only when the surface is free of scratches, fingerprints and colour difference](img:20)

A final inspection before packing: the surface must be free of scratches, fingerprints and colour difference, and joint dimensions must match the fabrication ticket. Wipe with neutral detergent and blow off dust with compressed air so no swarf or dust remains.

- **Sub-operations** — surface check → dimension re-check → cleaning → drying
- **Control points** — no visible surface defects; fingerprints and oil fully removed; packing starts only after the piece passes inspection

### 3.2 Surface Film Application

![Surface film application in the field: the film advances slowly from one end, squeegeed flat, flush with or inside the panel edge, retention ≤ 30 days](img:21)

Mirror and PVD titanium parts get a PE protective film or electrostatic film first, then a layer of non-woven fabric. Brushed parts may take PE film only. Advance the film slowly from one end, squeegee out the air and avoid creases.

- **Sub-operations** — clean surface → apply PE film → squeegee flat → add non-woven layer → trim edges
- **Control points** — film free of bubbles and creases; film flush with or slightly inside the panel edge; retention ≤ 30 days

### 3.3 Corner Protection

![Corner protection in the field: plastic protectors ≥ 10 mm covering all four corners, L-edge guards on the long edges, tape never touching the film](img:22)

Fit plastic or pearl-cotton corner protectors on all four corners and L-shaped edge guards along the long edges. Protector thickness ≥ 10 mm, fixed with tape. This step is the key defence against knocks in transit, especially for mirror and shaped parts.

- **Sub-operations** — fit four corner protectors → fit L-edge guards → tape fixing → check firmness
- **Control points** — protector thickness ≥ 10 mm; all four corners covered; tape never sticks directly to the protective film

### 3.4 Cushioning Wrap

![Cushioning wrap in the field: 2–3 full layers of pearl cotton / bubble wrap, seams overlapping ≥ 50 mm, custom slots for shaped parts](img:23)

Wrap the whole piece in 2–3 layers of pearl cotton or bubble wrap with seams overlapping ≥ 50 mm, taped down. Shaped parts need custom pearl-cotton slots moulded in one piece so nothing shifts in transit.

- **Sub-operations** — base layer → middle layer → face layer → seam fixing → shaped-part slots
- **Control points** — at least 2 wrap layers; seams overlap ≥ 50 mm; no unsupported overhang on shaped parts

### 3.5 Layer Separation

![Layer separation in the field: pearl cotton / honeycomb separators ≥ 5 mm fully covering between panels — metal never touches metal](img:24)

When one crate carries several panels, a separator — pearl cotton sheet or honeycomb board ≥ 5 mm — must go between every pair. Metal panels must never touch; micro-vibration in transit would scratch the surfaces.

- **Sub-operations** — measure panel spacing → cut separators → place separators → check coverage
- **Control points** — separators fully cover between panels; separator size ≥ panel size; never omitted

### 3.6 Inner Boxing

![Inner boxing in the field: panels loaded vertically with void-free filling all round and a 20–30 mm cushioning layer to the crate walls](img:25)

Place the wrapped pieces into the crate or carton and fill all around with pearl cotton or foam board so no voids remain. Panels stand vertically with a 20–30 mm cushioning layer to the crate walls.

- **Sub-operations** — base padding → load panels → fill the sides → top padding → check for voids
- **Control points** — no voids in the crate; panels vertical; 20–30 mm cushioning layer to the crate walls

### 3.7 Structural Fixing

![Structural fixing in the field: timber braces with angle brackets and padded contact faces, mid-span supports on long pieces to prevent bending](img:26)

Brace the panels inside the crate with timber battens or a steel frame so they cannot shift in transit. Pad every batten-to-panel contact face with pearl cotton. Extra mid-span supports are added to extra-long pieces to prevent bending.

- **Sub-operations** — fit timber braces → fix angle brackets → pad contact faces → add mid-span supports
- **Control points** — no room for panels to shift; mid-span support on long pieces; cushioning on every contact face

### 3.8 Closing and Strapping

![Closing and strapping in the field: lid screwed down, 2–3 straps each way locked tight, even tension without harming the crate surface](img:27)

Fix the crate lid with self-tapping screws or steel nails; seal cartons with packing tape. Then run PP or PET strapping 2–3 passes lengthwise and crosswise, locked with buckles at even tension.

- **Sub-operations** — fix the lid → lengthwise straps → crosswise straps → lock buckles → check tension
- **Control points** — even strap tension; 2–3 passes each way; no damage to the crate surface

### 3.9 Labelling and QR Code

![Labelling and QR code in the field: QR label plus fragile / upright / moisture / gravity marks on the crate front, bilingual and wear-resistant](img:28)

Apply the QR-code label to the crate front — scan it for fabrication parameters, installation position and batch data. Add the upright, fragile, moisture and centre-of-gravity marks, all bilingual in Chinese and English.

- **Sub-operations** — print labels → apply QR code → apply fragile mark → apply upright mark → apply moisture mark
- **Control points** — QR data complete and scannable; marks correctly oriented; labels waterproof and wear-resistant

### 3.10 Weighing and Recording

![Weighing and recording in the field: every crate weighed with gross, net and dimensions recorded, ≤ 800 kg each, packing photos archived](img:29)

Weigh every crate, record gross and net weight and crate dimensions, and enter them into the logistics system. Keep each crate ≤ 800 kg — split it or switch to a steel frame if over. Photograph the packed crate as pre-transport evidence.

- **Sub-operations** — weighing → record dimensions → enter into system → photo archive
- **Control points** — ≤ 800 kg per crate; dimensions fully recorded; packing photos archived

### 3.11 Loading and Securing

![Loading and securing in the field: crates upright, gaps filled with timber and air dunnage bags, load strapped to the truck body, rain cover on](img:30)

Load crates upright — never inverted or on their side. Fill the gaps between crates with timber or air dunnage bags and secure them to the truck body with straps. Load in reverse order of unloading: the first crate off goes nearest the door.

- **Sub-operations** — clean the truck box → position crates → fill between crates → secure the load → rain cover
- **Control points** — crates upright; no gaps between crates; load firmly secured; protected from rain and moisture

### 3.12 Transport Tracking and Delivery

![Transport and delivery in the field: GPS tracking, outer packaging inspected and photographed on arrival, unloading follows the marks, signed acceptance on site](img:31)

Stay in contact with the driver and track GPS position in transit. On arrival, inspect the outer packaging for damage first and photograph it as evidence. Unload gently, carry in the direction the marks show, and never roll the crates.

- **Sub-operations** — GPS tracking → arrival inspection → photo evidence → unloading → signed acceptance
- **Control points** — outer packaging undamaged; unloading follows the marks; signed acceptance on site

> Key point: the twelve operations run in a fixed order because each one removes a risk the next one cannot fix. Clean before you film, film before you corner-guard, cushion before you close, and label before you weigh — reversing any step means doing the work twice.

## 7. Common Packing and Transport Defects and Prevention

These twelve problems account for over 90% of metalwork transport damage. Spotting the causes and defending during packing costs an order of magnitude less than reworking afterwards.

### 7.1 Surface Scratches

![Surface scratch defect in the field: no separators, broken film, panels shifting and rubbing in transit — prevention: separators ≥ 5 mm between panels, no movement in the crate](img:45)

No separators between panels, broken protective film, or panels shifting and rubbing in transit scratch mirror or brushed surfaces.

- **Prevention** — separators ≥ 5 mm between panels; protective film intact; panels fixed so they cannot shift

### 7.2 Corner Damage

![Corner damage defect in the field: missing or thin protectors, rolling and knocks during unloading — prevention: corner protectors ≥ 10 mm, carry by the marks](img:46)

Missing or too-thin corner protectors, crates rolled during unloading, or crates knocking during loading dent or deform the corners.

- **Prevention** — corner protectors ≥ 10 mm on all four corners; carry in the direction the marks show; fill between crates when loading

### 7.3 Surface Dents

![Surface dent defect in the field: over-stacking, weight on crates, insufficient cushioning — prevention: control stacking layers, pearl cotton ≥ 20 mm](img:47)

Too many stacking layers, heavy objects on top of crates, or insufficient cushion thickness press dents into the panel surface.

- **Prevention** — strictly control stacking layers; never put weight on crates; cushion thickness to spec (pearl cotton ≥ 20 mm)

### 7.4 Bending Deformation

![Bending defect in the field: no mid-span support on long pieces, uneven forces in horizontal transport — prevention: mid-span supports, transport vertically](img:48)

No mid-span support on extra-long pieces, horizontal placement, or uneven forces in transit bow the panels.

- **Prevention** — add mid-span supports on long pieces; transport vertically; brace with timber battens

### 7.5 Moisture and Rust

![Moisture and rust defect in the field: rain in transit, poor crate moisture protection, residual water — prevention: desiccant bags, rain-proof vehicles, pack only dry surfaces](img:49)

Rain in transit, insufficient moisture protection, or residual water on the metal surface causes rust or oxidation.

- **Prevention** — desiccant bags inside; waterproof the crate; rain-proof the vehicle; pack only fully dried surfaces

### 7.6 Adhesive Residue

![Adhesive residue defect in the field: film kept over 30 days, heat exposure, low-grade film — prevention: retain ≤ 30 days, low-tack quality PE or electrostatic film](img:50)

Film kept over 30 days, high-temperature exposure, or low-grade film leaves adhesive residue when peeled.

- **Prevention** — retain film ≤ 30 days; avoid high-temperature exposure; choose low-tack quality PE film or electrostatic film

### 7.7 Label Loss

![Label loss defect in the field: non-waterproof labels, poor adhesion, rubbing in transit — prevention: waterproof wear-resistant labels, clean the surface first](img:51)

Non-waterproof labels, poor adhesion or rubbing in transit makes QR codes and marks fall off, breaking traceability.

- **Prevention** — use waterproof wear-resistant labels; clean the crate surface before applying; over-tape critical marks with clear tape

### 7.8 Unpacking Damage

![Unpacking damage in the field: knife scoring the surface, forced opening, film peeled too fast — prevention: proper tools, control the blade angle, peel slowly](img:52)

Utility knives scratching the surface, forced opening, or peeling the film too fast leaves residue and damage.

- **Prevention** — open with proper tools; control the blade angle so it never scores the surface; peel the film slowly

### 7.9 Strap Marks

![Strap mark defect in the field: over-tension, strap on bare surface, no pads — prevention: moderate tension, protector pads on contact faces, wider straps](img:53)

Over-tensioned straps, straps running directly on the metal, or missing protector pads press strap marks into the surface.

- **Prevention** — moderate strap tension; pad every contact face; use wider straps to spread the pressure

### 7.10 Crate Damage

![Crate damage defect in the field: plywood too thin, over-tensioned straps, impacts in transit — prevention: panels ≥ 12 mm, even tension, fill between crates](img:54)

Plywood too thin, over-tensioned straps, or impacts in transit crack or deform the crate.

- **Prevention** — plywood ≥ 12 mm; even strap tension; fill between crates when loading

### 7.11 Water Trapped Under Film

![Water under film defect in the field: unsealed edges, rain entering in transit causing oxidation — prevention: press edges down, waterproof crates, inspect on arrival](img:55)

Unsealed film edges and rain in transit let moisture under the film, oxidising or rusting the metal.

- **Prevention** — press film edges down firmly; waterproof the crate; rain-proof the vehicle; inspect promptly on arrival

### 7.12 QR Code Failure

![QR failure defect in the field: poor print quality, abraded labels, bad scan angle — prevention: high-precision printing, back up data on the packing list](img:56)

Poor print quality, abraded labels or a bad scan angle make the code unreadable.

- **Prevention** — high-precision printing; waterproof wear-resistant labels; back up critical data on the packing list

> Key point: ten of the twelve defects are decided before the crate leaves the workshop. Only label loss and QR failure happen on the road — which is why the packing inspection list is signed before loading, not after unloading.

## 9. Packing Environment and Safety Rules

Packing involves cutting, strapping and handling, so the work environment and personal safety both need attention. Environmentally sound disposal of packaging materials is also part of project delivery.

### 9.1 Environment · Working Area

![Working area in the field: 15–30 ℃ · 40–65% RH · lighting ≥ 300 lux · level dry dust-free floor · aisles ≥ 1.2 m · fire equipment in place](img:66)

Environmental requirements for the packing area.

- **Temperature** — 15–30 ℃
- **Humidity** — 40–65% RH (moisture control)
- **Lighting** — ≥ 300 lux (for inspection)
- **Floor** — level, dry, dust-free
- **Aisles** — width ≥ 1.2 m
- **Fire** — extinguishers, no-smoking signs

### 9.2 Safety · Personnel

![Personnel safety in the field: cut-resistant gloves · goggles · safety shoes · dust masks · ≤ 25 kg solo handling, two people or a forklift above · trained operators](img:67)

Protective equipment every operator must have.

- **Gloves** — cut-resistant (for cutting work)
- **Goggles** — against splash
- **Safety shoes** — anti-crush, anti-puncture
- **Masks** — dust (foam cutting)
- **Handling** — ≤ 25 kg solo; two people or a forklift above
- **Training** — equipment operation training

### 9.3 Recycling · Disposal

![Recycling in the field: cartons / crates / frames recovered and reused · pearl cotton specialist recycling · film sorted · desiccant compliant disposal](img:68)

Recycling and environmental requirements for packaging materials.

- **Cartons** — recyclable, sorted on site
- **Crates** — recyclable, reused
- **Frames** — recyclable, returned to circulation
- **Pearl cotton** — recyclable, specialist processing
- **Protective film** — sorted recycling, never dumped at will
- **Desiccant** — compliant disposal, never landfilled

> Environmental principle: packaging materials should favour types that are recyclable, degradable and reusable. Structural materials such as steel frames and crates should be recovered and reused — it cuts cost and site waste alike. Film and pearl cotton must be sorted for recycling, never dumped or burned, and all packaging waste must be handled in line with local environmental regulations with disposal records kept.

---

Packing and crating is the last step before the metalwork leaves the workshop and the first thing the site sees on arrival. Done properly, it brings the transport damage rate from 12.5% down to under 1.0%, keeps every panel traceable by QR code, and closes the quality loop that fabrication opened.

Process parameters are industry reference values; actual projects are governed by the developed drawings, sealed samples and the transport plan.
