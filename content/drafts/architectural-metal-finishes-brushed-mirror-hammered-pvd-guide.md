A showroom finish swatch gives the design team a colour reference. The installed surface adds panel joints, curved returns, overhead lighting and cleaning access. Approve a sample with those conditions represented before releasing architectural metalwork for manufacture. For a display cabinet, that may mean a lit corner with the actual glass. For a cladding package, it may mean two adjoining panels at the proposed radius.

## Separate the alloy, texture and colour

Record the base metal, the mechanical finish and the colour or protection system as separate items on the finish schedule. For example, a written brief might request stainless steel of the grade shown on the metalwork schedule, a directional brushed finish and an approved champagne PVD colour. Give the reference sample a revision number and link it to the visible faces on the drawing. Ask the supplier to identify any clear topcoat or surface treatment that affects cleaning and future repair.

Start the visual review with a panel corner or junction. Hammered texture changes the size and direction of highlights, making the appearance at a brass mitre different from the appearance on a small flat sample. Inspect a [hotel lobby vitrine workshop trial](/case-studies/luxury-hotel-lobby-brass-vitrines-ffe) for an example of a textured face meeting a glazed display opening.

## Compare brushed, mirror and textured finishes in place

### Brushed and satin finishes

On a brushed surface, the visible grain can make the same sheet look lighter when seen from one direction and darker from another. Draw the grain direction on each panel and check two adjoining modules under light that falls at a shallow angle. Where a seam is welded and dressed, include a representative portion of it in the sample. [worldstainless surface treatment guide](https://worldstainless.org/about-stainless/process-and-production/surface-treatment/)

### Mirror-polished finishes

A mirror finish makes the reflection part of the design. View a full-size sample section from the entrance and along the wall to judge how broad reflected lines cross the joints. For a formed panel, inspect the bend and the finished edge. The buyer can then agree where a sharper reflection matters and where a satin finish suits the sightline. [worldstainless surface treatment guide](https://worldstainless.org/about-stainless/process-and-production/surface-treatment/)

### Hammered and fluted finishes

Hammered metal adds small-scale texture. Flutes add a larger relief whose spacing should read continuously around a corner. On a counter, review the rhythm at the last flute, the interruption at a recess and the meeting line at the worktop. On an atrium soffit, add a low-angle view of the return to the approval set.

## Approve PVD over the intended surface

Physical vapour deposition (PVD) deposits a thin coloured coating, and the finish below remains visible. The same nominal colour can read differently over mirror and brushed bases. Ask for a colour range on the proposed substrate and have the fabricator document the sequence for cutting, forming, joining and coating. If any piece will be formed after coating, agree a representative formed sample. Confirm the intended cleaning method and how damaged modules would be replaced. [International Molybdenum Association guidance on coloured stainless steel](https://www.imoa.info/stainless-solutions/archive/18/colored-stainless-steel.php?m=1683978049)

Compare junctions using [the satin stainless column workshop trial](/case-studies/murray-road-column-cladding) and [the fluted escalator cladding case](/case-studies/curved-fluted-escalator-atrium-cladding). The photos show where grain, shape and seam appearance are reviewed. Coating type and alloy grade should be recorded on the material submittal.

## Use this finish approval checklist

1. Identify the base alloy, the visible finish and any colour layer or topcoat by drawing reference.
2. Approve two adjacent panels and one representative corner, bend or welded junction under the intended light.
3. Mark the viewing distance, grain direction, exposed edges and acceptable variation on the approval record.
4. Agree how the client will clean the surface and how the installer will protect it during handling.
5. Keep a signed control sample and specify matching requirements for spare panels.
