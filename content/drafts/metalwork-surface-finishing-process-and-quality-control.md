Luxury boutiques, high-end residences and luxury hotels ask very different things of metal surface finishing, yet they share one underlying logic: pre-treatment decides adhesion, process parameters decide texture, and process control decides batch consistency. This guide breaks spray coating, polishing, brushing and electroplating down to the sub-process level, with a scene selection matrix, parameter gradients, equipment configuration, inspection methods and defect prevention.

## 1. The Four Surface Treatment Processes at a Glance

Spray for colour, polishing for gloss, brushing for texture, plating for performance. The four processes can be used alone or stacked in combination — selection depends on the base material, the scene and the budget, but the pre-treatment logic and quality-control principles are the same.

### 1.1 Spray Coating

![Spray booth in the field: an operator spraying an aluminium panel with an electrostatic gun](img:01)

Atomised paint is sprayed onto the metal surface to form a continuous coating. Strong colour capability across the full matte-to-gloss range — the workhorse process for aluminium panels and shaped parts.

- **Film** — 30–120 μm
- **Colour difference** — ΔE ≤ 1.0
- **Best for** — aluminium / shaped parts

### 1.2 Polishing

![8K mirror-polished stainless steel: distortion-free reflection](img:02)

Microscopic peaks are removed mechanically or chemically to lower roughness. From matte satin to an 8K mirror, the polish grade directly decides the metal's sense of quality.

- **Roughness** — Ra 0.01–0.8 μm
- **Gloss** — 12–96 GU
- **Best for** — stainless / copper

### 1.3 Brushing

![Long-grain brushed stainless steel panel: directional hairline texture](img:03)

Abrasives scratch the surface in a controlled direction to form a regular pattern. Brushing hides fine scratches, cuts reflection and adds tactile depth — the most widely used metal texture process for residences and hotels.

- **Roughness** — Ra 0.2–1.2 μm
- **Texture** — long / short / snow grain
- **Best for** — all scenes

### 1.4 Electroplating / PVD

![PVD gold titanium coated panel: hard metallic colour finish](img:04)

A metal layer is deposited by electrolysis or vacuum coating. PVD titanium coating gives gold, black titanium, rose gold and other colours with high hardness and wear and corrosion resistance — the first choice for luxury boutiques.

- **Film** — 0.3–0.5 μm
- **Hardness** — HV 1500–2500
- **Best for** — retail / hotel

> Key point: the four processes are not interchangeable. Spray changes colour, polish changes gloss, brush changes texture and plate changes performance — pick the property you actually need before you pick the process.

## 3. Spray Coating: The Complete Process

The heart of spraying is not the spraying but the pre-treatment and curing. Coating adhesion, colour difference and weatherability depend seven-tenths on thorough pre-treatment and a precise cure curve. Broken down below from substrate pre-treatment to finished-part inspection.

### 3.1 Process Flow

Atomised paint is attached evenly to the metal surface, then levelled and cured into a continuous coating. Suited to aluminium panels, shaped parts and large facades, with the full colour range from matte to gloss and from plain to metallic. Common types: fluorocarbon (PVDF), powder and ceramic coating.

- **Step 01 · Substrate pre-treatment** — degrease → derust → chromate
- **Step 02 · Primer spray** — film 5–10 μm
- **Step 03 · Levelling & flash-off** — room temperature, 10–15 min
- **Step 04 · Topcoat spray** — film 25–40 μm
- **Step 05 · High-temp cure** — 180–250 ℃ / 15–25 min
- **Step 06 · Cooling & inspection** — ΔE / film / adhesion

### 3.2 Sub-Process Breakdown

**Pre-treatment**

- **01-1 Alkaline degreasing** — alkaline degreaser pH 9–11 · 50–60 ℃ · soak / spray 3–5 min · rinse to conductivity ≤ 50 μS/cm
- **01-2 Acid pickling** — nitric / sulfuric mixed acid · room temperature 2–5 min · removes scale and rust · two rinse stages
- **01-3 Chromating / chromate-free passivation** — chromate bath Cr³⁺ 0.5–2 g/L · 25–35 ℃ · 1–3 min · forms a nanoscale conversion film
- **01-4 DI-water rinse & drying** — DI-water rinse conductivity ≤ 10 μS/cm · drying 80–120 ℃ / 10–15 min · no water marks · into the dust-free booth

**Primer**

- **02-1 Primer spray** — epoxy primer film 5–10 μm · gun distance 150–250 mm · atomising pressure 0.3–0.5 MPa · even coverage, no misses

**Topcoat**

- **04-1 Topcoat spray** — fluorocarbon / powder film 25–40 μm · spray environment 20–28 ℃ / 45–65% RH · thin multi-pass spraying 2–3 passes · colour difference ΔE ≤ 1.0

### 3.3 Key Parameters

- Pre-treatment — chromating / chromate-free passivation
- Degrease temperature — 50–60 ℃
- DI-water conductivity — ≤ 10 μS/cm
- Spray environment — 20–28 ℃ · 45–65% RH
- Gun distance — 150–250 mm
- Atomising pressure — 0.3–0.5 MPa
- Total film — 30–120 μm
- Cure temperature — 180–250 ℃
- Cure time — 15–25 min
- Adhesion — cross-cut class 0 / ≥ 95% squares
- Weatherability — QUV ≥ 2000 h
- Pencil hardness — ≥ 2H

### 3.4 Control Points

- **Pre-treatment is everything** — incomplete degreasing causes blistering and peeling. The line must run degrease → rinse → chromate → DI rinse with conductivity ≤ 10 μS/cm.
- **Layered film control** — primer 5–10 μm and topcoat 25–40 μm; too thick sags, too thin lacks hiding power.
- **Same-batch colour control** — one colour code must be sprayed in one batch, with every panel colorimeter-checked to ΔE ≤ 1.0.
- **Cure-curve records** — oven temperature curves must be archived; too fast a ramp causes pinholes and orange peel.
- **Booth cleanliness** — the booth must be positive-pressure dust-free with particles ≤ 1000/m³, or the coating shows specks.
- **Jig management** — jigs need regular cleaning; residual coating affects conductivity and earthing, giving uneven film build.

### 3.5 Equipment

- **Pre-treatment line** — degreasing / pickling tanks · chromate / DI-rinse tanks · drying oven
- **Spray equipment** — electrostatic / air spray guns · reciprocators / robots · positive-pressure dust-free booth
- **Curing equipment** — tunnel curing oven · oven temperature tracker · cooling zone

## 4. Polishing: The Complete Process

Polishing is a subtractive process — microscopic peaks are removed by step-by-step grinding. From coarse grinding to mirror, every grade must fully remove the previous grade's marks or a haze remains. Polish grades from matte to 8K mirror directly decide the metal's sense of quality.

### 4.1 Process Flow

Abrasives or chemical action lower the surface roughness to give a mirror or satin finish. Mechanical polishing steps through belts, sisal wheels and cloth wheels; electrolytic polishing dissolves electrochemically for a uniform mirror.

- **Step 01 · Coarse grind** — #120–#240 belt
- **Step 02 · Medium grind** — #400–#800 belt
- **Step 03 · Fine grind** — #1000–#2000
- **Step 04 · Sisal buffing** — compound W20–W10
- **Step 05 · Cloth-wheel finishing** — compound W5–W1
- **Step 06 · Cleaning & inspection** — Ra / gloss

### 4.2 Sub-Process Breakdown

- **01-1 Coarse grinding** — belt #120–#240 · belt speed 15–25 m/s · removes weld marks and deep scratches · direction perpendicular to the next grade
- **02-1 Medium grinding** — belt #400–#800 · pressure 0.2–0.5 MPa · removes coarse-grind marks · even surface, no patches
- **03-1 Fine grinding** — belt / paper #1000–#2000 · wet or oil grinding · uniform matte surface · prepares for buffing
- **04-1 Sisal buffing** — sisal wheel Ø 200–300 mm · speed 2000–2500 rpm · compound W20–W10 · removes fine-grind marks
- **05-1 Cloth-wheel finishing** — cloth wheel Ø 250–350 mm · speed 2500–3000 rpm · compound W5–W1 · achieves the mirror
- **06-1 Cleaning & inspection** — ultrasonic cleaning with neutral detergent · profilometer Ra check · gloss meter GU check · visual check for haze / rings

### 4.3 Key Parameters

- Coarse grit — #120 – #240
- Medium grit — #400 – #800
- Fine grit — #1000 – #2000
- Rough buffing speed — 2000–2500 rpm
- Finishing buffing speed — 2500–3000 rpm
- Compound grade — W20 – W1
- Buffing pressure — 0.2–0.5 MPa
- Final roughness — Ra 0.01–0.8 μm
- Gloss — 12–96 GU
- Flatness — ≤ 0.5 mm/m
- Surface temperature — ≤ 80 ℃
- Electrolytic current density — 20–50 A/dm²

### 4.4 Control Points

- **Step through every grit** — each new belt must fully remove the previous grade's marks, or the mirror shows haze or rings.
- **Alternate direction** — grind each grade perpendicular to the last so you can see at a glance whether it is ground through.
- **Temperature control** — excessive pressure overheats the surface locally, yellowing stainless steel and oxidising copper.
- **Electropolishing** — ideal for complex shaped parts and gives a uniform mirror, but current density and electrolyte temperature must be controlled.
- **Buffing wheel care** — cloth wheels need regular dressing; an uneven wheel face causes waviness.
- **Clean immediately** — clean straight after polishing; residual compound corrodes the surface.

### 4.5 Equipment

- **Grinding equipment** — wide-belt sander · surface grinder · belt grinder
- **Polishing equipment** — buffing lathes / automatic lines · sisal / cloth / wool wheels · automatic compound spraying
- **Electropolishing** — electrolytic tank / rectifier · temperature control system · cathode plates / jigs

## 5. Brushing: The Complete Process

Brushing forms texture by directional abrasive scratching. Grain direction, coarseness and depth decide the final look — long grain is continuous, short grain is fine, snow-fine gives a random pattern. Brushing hides fine scratches, cuts reflection and adds tactile depth.

### 5.1 Process Flow

Belts, non-woven pads or wire brushes scratch the metal surface directionally to form regular or random micro-texture — the most widely used metal texture process for premium residences and hotels. Grain grades include coarse, fine, long, short and snow-fine.

- **Step 01 · Substrate cleaning** — degrease / dust off
- **Step 02 · Coarse grain setting** — #120–#240 belt
- **Step 03 · Fine grain refining** — #320–#600 belt
- **Step 04 · Grain unifying** — non-woven pad / nylon wheel
- **Step 05 · Surface cleaning** — remove swarf
- **Step 06 · Film protection** — PE film / AF coating

### 5.2 Sub-Process Breakdown

- **01-1 Substrate cleaning** — wipe with neutral detergent · removes oil and fingerprints · compressed-air dusting · surface dry, no water marks
- **02-1 Coarse grain setting** — belt #120–#240 · belt speed 12–20 m/s · feed rate 3–8 m/min · sets grain direction and depth
- **03-1 Fine grain refining** — belt #320–#600 · lowers surface roughness · finer, more even grain · direction matches the coarse pass
- **04-1 Grain unifying** — non-woven pad / nylon wheel · removes belt seams · grain depth 0.5–3 μm · gloss 30–55 GU
- **05-1 Surface cleaning** — removes swarf and dust · wipe with neutral detergent · blown dry with compressed air · check grain evenness
- **06-1 Film protection** — PE protective film 50–80 μm · anti-fingerprint coating 1–3 μm · corner guards on all four edges · avoid stacking damage

### 5.3 Key Parameters

- Coarse grit — #120 – #240
- Fine grit — #320 – #600
- Belt speed — 12–20 m/s
- Feed speed — 3–8 m/min
- Grain depth — 0.5–3 μm
- Roughness — Ra 0.2–1.2 μm
- Gloss — 30–55 GU
- Grain direction — long / short / random
- Anti-fingerprint coating — 1–3 μm
- Coating hardness — ≥ 3H

### 5.4 Control Points

- **One grain direction** — every panel on the same elevation must be brushed in the same direction, or reflections clash badly at different angles.
- **Seam transition** — grain on adjacent panels must flow across the joint; the texture must not break at the seam.
- **Belt wear management** — belts must be replaced at end of life, or the grain gets shallower and colour difference appears.
- **Anti-fingerprint coating** — an AF coating after brushing stops fingerprints — standard for premium residences and hotels.
- **Even pressure** — brushing pressure must stay constant; pressure fluctuation gives uneven grain depth.
- **Clean thoroughly** — all swarf must be removed after brushing; residual dust harms later coating adhesion.

### 5.5 Equipment

- **Brushing equipment** — wide-belt brushing machine · flat-panel brushing machine · automatic brushing line
- **Abrasives & consumables** — belts #120–#600 · non-woven pad / nylon wheel · wire brushes / grinding wheels
- **Coating equipment** — AF coating spray line · baking oven · film applicator

## 6. Electroplating & PVD: The Complete Process

Electroplating deposits a functional layer by electrolysis or vacuum coating. PVD titanium coating gives gold, black titanium, rose gold and other colours with high hardness, wear and corrosion resistance — the first choice for luxury boutiques. Broken down below along the wet-plating and vacuum-coating routes.

### 6.1 Process Flow

Plating splits into wet electroplating and vacuum coating (PVD). PVD titanium is deposited by target sputtering in vacuum: film 0.3–0.5 μm, hardness HV 1500–2500, in gold, champagne, black titanium, rose gold and blue titanium. Anodising is mainly for aluminium, giving a wear- and weather-resistant oxide film.

- **Step 01 · Pre-treatment cleaning** — degrease → pickle → DI rinse
- **Step 02 · Drying & loading** — dust-free / dew-point control
- **Step 03 · Ion bombardment** — Ar ion cleaning
- **Step 04 · Film deposition** — target sputtering / arc evaporation
- **Step 05 · Cooling & unloading** — vacuum-cool to 60 ℃
- **Step 06 · Inspection & film** — film / ΔE / adhesion

### 6.2 Sub-Process Breakdown

**Pre-treatment**

- **01-1 Ultrasonic degreasing** — alkaline degreaser pH 9–11 · 50–60 ℃ · ultrasonic 3–5 min · two rinse stages
- **01-2 Acid activation** — weak-acid soak 1–3 min · removes the oxide film · activates the surface · DI-water rinse
- **01-3 DI-water rinse & drying** — DI-water rinse conductivity ≤ 10 μS/cm · drying 80–120 ℃ · transfer in dust-free conditions · dew point ≤ −40 ℃

**Coating**

- **03-1 Ion bombardment cleaning** — Ar ion bombardment · vacuum level ≤ 5×10⁻³ Pa · bias voltage 300–800 V · time 5–15 min
- **04-1 Film deposition** — targets Ti / Cr / Zr · arc current 60–120 A · deposition temperature 150–450 ℃ · film 0.3–0.5 μm
- **06-1 Inspection & film** — thickness gauge 0.3–0.5 μm · colorimeter ΔE ≤ 1.0 · cross-cut adhesion class 0 · PE film protection

### 6.3 Key Parameters

- Pre-treatment conductivity — ≤ 10 μS/cm
- Vacuum level — ≤ 5×10⁻³ Pa
- Ion-bombardment bias — 300–800 V
- Arc current — 60–120 A
- Coating temperature — 150–450 ℃
- Film — 0.3–0.5 μm
- Hardness — HV 1500–2500
- Adhesion — cross-cut class 0
- Salt spray — ≥ 500 h
- Colour difference — ΔE ≤ 1.0
- Anodised film — 10–25 μm
- Anodising voltage — 12–20 V

### 6.4 Control Points

- **Pre-treatment decides adhesion** — eight out of ten PVD failures come from incomplete pre-treatment. Multi-stage ultrasonic cleaning is mandatory, with DI-rinse conductivity ≤ 10 μS/cm.
- **One furnace, one colour** — one colour code must be coated in one furnace run; target wear shifts colour, so targets need scheduled replacement.
- **Film uniformity** — parts must rotate in the chamber so all faces coat evenly, deviation ≤ ±15%.
- **Colour-difference control** — keep a colour board per furnace and colorimeter-sample panels; deliver only at ΔE ≤ 1.0.
- **Target management** — targets must be replaced at end of life, or film composition drifts and colour shifts.
- **Cooling rate** — vacuum-cool below 60 ℃ before opening the furnace; quenching cracks the film.

### 6.5 Equipment

- **Pre-treatment equipment** — ultrasonic cleaning line · DI-rinse tank · drying oven
- **Vacuum coating equipment** — multi-arc ion plating furnace · magnetron sputtering furnace · vacuum pump set
- **Anodising equipment** — electrolytic tank / rectifier · temperature control system · sealing line

## 9. Common Surface Treatment Defects and Prevention

These twelve problems account for over 90% of after-sales complaints in metal surface finishing. Spotting the causes and defending early in the process costs an order of magnitude less than reworking afterwards.

### 9.1 Coating peeling (spray)

Incomplete degreasing, an incomplete chromate film, or insufficient cure temperature or time leave adhesion below spec.

- **Prevention** — multi-stage ultrasonic cleaning with DI-rinse conductivity ≤ 10 μS/cm; cure curves recorded and archived; cross-cut class 0 before delivery

### 9.2 Orange peel / sagging (spray)

Gun too close, film too thick, thinner flashing off too fast, or ambient temperature too high — the coating levels poorly.

- **Prevention** — gun distance 150–250 mm; control film by multi-pass spraying; 20–28 ℃ and 45–65% RH

### 9.3 Specks / contamination (spray)

Poor booth cleanliness, jigs shedding old coating, or poorly filtered paint put particles in the film.

- **Prevention** — positive-pressure dust-free booth with particles ≤ 1000/m³; clean jigs regularly; filter paint through 100–150 mesh

### 9.4 Haze / rings (polish)

Previous-grade marks not fully removed when changing grits, or wheel speed and pressure too high, overheating the surface locally.

- **Prevention** — step through every grit with direction perpendicular each time; keep speed at 2000–3000 rpm; reflection-test mirrors

### 9.5 Surface yellowing (polish)

Excessive pressure overheats the surface and oxidises stainless yellow; copper not oxidation-protected straight after polishing.

- **Prevention** — control pressure and temperature; apply AF coating or anti-oxidation sealing immediately after polishing

### 9.6 Waviness (polish)

An uneven wheel face or an undressed cloth wheel puts periodic waves in the surface.

- **Prevention** — dress wheel faces regularly; replace cloth wheels at end of life; keep buffing pressure even

### 9.7 Mismatched grain direction (brush)

Panels on the same elevation brushed in different directions, or grain not flowing across joints — reflections clash badly at different angles.

- **Prevention** — mark grain direction on the fabrication ticket; one direction per elevation; grain must flow across seams

### 9.8 Uneven grain depth (brush)

Belts used past their life give shallower grain; unstable feed speed gives uneven depth.

- **Prevention** — log belt hours and replace on schedule; hold feed speed at 3–8 m/min; compare grain depth panel by panel

### 9.9 Fingerprint residue (brush)

No AF coating after brushing, or the coating is too thin, so fingerprints will not wipe off.

- **Prevention** — apply 1–3 μm AF coating after brushing; cure it fully; supply neutral detergent and microfibre cloths

### 9.10 Film peeling (PVD)

Incomplete pre-treatment cleaning, insufficient vacuum, film too thin, or later attack by strong-alkali or chlorine cleaners.

- **Prevention** — multi-stage ultrasonic cleaning with conductivity ≤ 10 μS/cm; vacuum ≤ 5×10⁻³ Pa; film ≥ 0.3 μm; supply a neutral-cleaner list

### 9.11 Colour drift (PVD)

Different target wear between furnace runs, temperature fluctuation, or part position in the chamber shift the colour.

- **Prevention** — one colour code per furnace run; replace targets on schedule; rotate parts for uniformity; keep a colour board per furnace at ΔE ≤ 1.0

### 9.12 Uneven film thickness (PVD)

Parts not rotating, unequal target-to-part distances, or arc-current fluctuation give uneven film across faces.

- **Prevention** — rotating jigs; control target-to-part distance; stable arc current; film deviation ≤ ±15%

> Core principle — the final result of surface finishing is decided 70% by pre-treatment, 20% by process parameters and 10% by equipment precision. Spray adhesion, plating bond, mirror quality and brushing uniformity all trace back to thorough pre-treatment and stable parameters. Pre-treatment must be standardised, cure curves recorded and archived, and batch colour difference instrument-checked — all three are indispensable.
