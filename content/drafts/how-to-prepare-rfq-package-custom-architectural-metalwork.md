A useful RFQ lets a fabricator work out what to make, how it meets the building and how it can be shipped. It also makes assumptions visible so competing tender prices can be compared. Send the current drawing set, sample brief, site envelope and delivery information together. List open decisions with their owners and due dates so tenderers price them on the same basis.

## Issue a dimensioned drawing package

Start with a drawing register showing revision numbers. Include plans, elevations, key sections and details at connections to stone, glass, timber or the main structure. If the metal surface has compound curvature, include the current model with units and reference axes. Make clear which dimensions on drawings or survey records govern if the model and the drawings disagree.

![Bare fluted escalator casing on a bench showing the formed sheet and its edge](img:1)

A curved enclosure needs more than its overall width and height. Show the panel breaks, changes in section, return depths and support points. For a stair or balustrade, include landing levels, profile sections and the start and finish of the rail. A visual reference helps communicate intent. A dimensioned section gives the fabricator something to price and check.

![Formed rail sections laid out in the workshop before fit-up](img:2)

In a finish schedule, identify each visible metal face, the proposed base metal if known, the grain direction and any surface treatment. Put the schedule reference on the drawings so it is clear which parts it applies to.

## Define what the sample must prove

Photographs and colour codes are a good starting point. Ask bidders to price the sample that will settle the decision. A small finish coupon may be enough for colour. A formed corner or assembled bay is more useful for checking brushing across returns, the appearance of a seam or reflection where the curvature changes.

![A hammered brass display case lit for a workshop check before delivery](img:3)

For lighting integrated into metalwork, state who supplies the light source, driver and diffuser and who connects them on site. Include the expected viewing distance and whether you want the sample checked lit and unlit. Ask the bidder to identify where the driver can be accessed after the surrounding work is closed. If the sample must include glazing, stone or a door that opens, show that boundary in the brief.

## Supply the site envelope and the delivery route

Provide a marked-up survey or identify the dimensions still to be verified. Show finished floor levels, soffits, floor openings and fixed obstructions. State where the metal package meets other suppliers and whether adjustable connections are available. For an escalator casing, include the equipment supplier’s clearance and access zones. For a lobby display case, show plinth fixings and the space needed for doors or service panels.

![A workshop floor layout supporting adjoining curved balustrade sections](img:4)

Record the usable dimensions of the service entrance, lift and tightest turn on the route to the final position. Include handling restrictions, loading access and where crates can be opened. These details can affect the position of module joints and the sequence of assembly. Ask how exposed edges, finished faces and glass will be protected. The existing [fit-out guide’s packing and logistics section](/blog/metalwork-assembly-fit-out-process-and-quality-control#step-06-film-packing-and-logistics) covers the packing questions to raise before dispatch.

## Ask each fabricator to return the same information

Use the same response checklist for every bidder so each price can be read against an agreed scope.

- **Scope and quantities.** Ask for items included, quantities, finish references and interfaces assigned to other trades.
- **Price basis and open decisions.** Ask which drawings were priced, what is provisional and how a change in grade, finish or module size would be handled.
- **Fabrication checks.** Request the proposed samples and trial extent, the points to be checked and the evidence you will receive before dispatch.
- **Packing and handling.** Ask for proposed module breaks and package sizes, lifting or handling notes and the packing scope included in the quote.
- **Programme and approvals.** Separate drawing approval, sample sign-off, fabrication and dispatch. Identify the decisions or dimensions the fabricator needs before starting each stage.
