Metal polishing and brushing is the stage where architectural metalwork earns its premium feel. From 80# abrasive-belt coarse grinding that removes weld scars, to 8000# diamond-paste mirror finishing; from 45° corner matching of brushed grain to film-thickness control in PVD vacuum coating — the look of luxury metal is never given by the material itself. It is earned by every process step, in millimetres and microns. This guide breaks down the ten process steps, equipment and consumables, cost and schedule, and the acceptance standards used for luxury boutiques, high-end residences and luxury hotels.

Headline control targets for the whole process:

- Core process steps: 10
- Abrasive grit range: 80# – 8000#
- Mirror surface roughness: Ra ≤ 0.012 μm
- Colour-difference cap: ΔE ≤ 1.5
- PVD coating hardness: 1500–2500 HV

## 1. Three Application Scenes and the Language of Surfaces

Different spaces read surfaces differently. A boutique is judged at one metre under spotlights, a residence is judged by hand, and a hotel lobby is judged in hundreds of square metres at once.

### 1.1 Luxury Boutique

Mirror stainless display cabinets, champagne-gold PVD door surrounds and brushed-copper logo walls. At close viewing distance, any orange peel, water ripple or fingerprint is magnified tenfold by the 3000 K spotlights.

### 1.2 High-End Residence

Etched stainless elevator lobbies, rose-gold trim lines and copper screens. These surfaces are touched every day — the grain must stay straight and fine, and the corners rounded and smooth.

## 2. The Ten Process Steps in Detail

The chain runs from sample approval to delivery: each step has its own abrasive range, equipment, targets and failure modes. Skipping or compressing a step is the root cause of nearly every visible defect later.

### 2.1 Step 01 — Sample approval (the benchmark)

![Sample plates of different finishes compared in a colour-matching light booth](img:10)

- Confirm the surface effect against the shop drawings: 8K mirror, long grain, short grain, frost sand, sandblast, etch, antique
- Produce a 1:1 300 × 300 mm sample panel; once the client signs and seals it, it becomes the single acceptance benchmark
- Confirm the PVD colour code, sealing requirements, installation direction and jointing method at the same time
- The sample panel must cover four states: flat surface, external corner, internal corner and weld-seam treatment
- **Panel quantity** — at least 3 per batch: one retained each by the client, the contractor and the fabricator
- **Storage** — keep away from light and moisture so the panel itself never discolours

> Key point: sign the sample first, then run the batch. Projects without a sealed sample always end in disputes.

### 2.2 Step 02 — Base-material inspection (source control)

![Quality inspector checking stacked stainless steel sheets with a micrometer](img:11)

- Sheet flatness ≤ 1.0 mm/m; surface free of scratches and pressure marks; protective film intact and undamaged
- Verify the same batch and the same heat number to avoid colour differences that cannot be repaired later
- Mirror parts should preferentially use fine-grain base material (grain size ≥ grade 8) to reduce the risk of polishing orange peel
- **304** — boutique cabinets, residential elevator lobbies, hotel interiors
- **316L** — hotel pools, spas, coastal projects, high-humidity zones
- **Brass H62** — brushed gold, aged copper, decorative trim
- **Copper T2** — antique bronze, art installations, lobby bars

> Key point: mixing sheets from different heat numbers is the most hidden — and most fatal — root of colour difference.

### 2.3 Step 03 — Cutting and forming

![Fiber laser cutting machine cutting a stainless steel sheet](img:12)

![CNC press brake bending a stainless steel sheet](img:13)

- Laser or waterjet cutting with cut-squareness ≤ 0.1 mm — no dross, no heat tint
- CNC bending with inner radius R ≥ sheet thickness to prevent the outer face cracking
- V-groove TIG welding at low current and fast travel; deslag and stress-relieve immediately after welding
- For thick plate (≥ 3 mm), laser or plasma welding is recommended — a smaller heat-affected zone
- **Bending direction** — keep bend lines perpendicular to the grain where possible
- **Welding sequence** — symmetric welding to reduce distortion and residual stress
- **Shielding gas** — argon purity ≥ 99.99%, flow 8–12 L/min
- **Post-weld treatment** — clean immediately with a stainless-dedicated wire brush to prevent intergranular corrosion

> Key point: excessive weld heat input means distortion plus intergranular corrosion — no amount of later grinding can save it.

### 2.4 Step 04 — Coarse grinding: de-scaling and levelling (80#–120#)

![Worker grinding down a weld bead with an angle grinder](img:14)

- Remove weld beads, spatter and misalignment; grind the weld reinforcement flush with the base metal, deviation ≤ 0.1 mm
- Grind in crossing passes to avoid one-directional deep grooves
- Even pressure and steady travel to prevent local hollows
- For large flat areas use a wide-belt sander — far better in both efficiency and flatness than hand work
- **Machine selection** — belt sander for flats, angle grinder for edges, die grinder for internal corners
- **Belt life** — replace zirconia belts every 15–25 ㎡, depending on pressure and material
- **Cooling** — dry grinding as standard; minimal coolant only when needed to prevent heat discolouration
- **Dust extraction** — industrial vacuum is mandatory; metal dust is flammable and explosive

> Key point: grooves left by coarse grinding are hard to fix later. The flatness of this step decides the flatness of the final mirror.

### 2.5 Step 05 — Medium grinding: shaping and transition (240#–400#)

![Worker blending a stainless surface with a flap disc](img:15)

- Step down grit by grit to remove the scratches left by coarse grinding
- Internal corners and radii must transition naturally — smooth to the touch, no ledges
- Break all exposed edges to prevent cuts during installation and use
- Inspect weld areas closely to ensure no micro-cracks or porosity remain
- **Radius standard** — exposed edges R ≥ 2 mm; R ≥ 3 mm in child-contact areas
- **Flap disc selection** — 240# for shaping, 400# for transition; radial runout ≤ 0.5 mm
- **Inspection method** — lightly reverse-wipe with 400# paper and check for residual coarse scratches
- **Common problem** — over-grinding edges causes "collapsed edges"; control pressure and dwell time

> Key point: the radius is where hand feel comes from — and the detail most often missed on premium projects.

### 2.6 Step 06 — Fine grinding: pattern removal (600#–800#)

![Worker cross-sanding a stainless panel with an orbital sander](img:16)

- Fully remove the previous pattern — "turn the direction and you see bare metal"
- Each grit runs 90° across the previous one, making it easy to judge whether you have ground through
- Focus on edges, weld seams and the area around screw holes
- Mirror parts must reach "no visible scratches, no heat tint" at this stage
- **Cross-grinding method** — rotate 90° at every grit change until the previous pattern has completely disappeared
- **Cleaning requirement** — clean thoroughly between every grit to prevent coarse grains scratching the surface
- **Inspection tools** — 10× loupe plus strong torch, viewed at 45° side light
- **Environment** — the fine-grinding area must be partitioned separately to avoid cross-contamination

> Key point: cross-grinding is the industry-standard way to verify that previous scratches are fully removed.

### 2.7 Step 07 — Fine finishing: brushing and mirror (1000#–8000#)

![Wide belt machine brushing long uniform grain into a stainless sheet](img:17)

- Brushing: one-directional passes; grain direction must be identical across the same face and matched at 45° on corners; straightness ≤ 1 mm/m, no waviness, no broken grain
- Mirror: progress 3000# → 5000# → 8000#, cleaning thoroughly at each stage to prevent coarse grains scratching the surface
- The fine-finishing area must be partitioned; temperature 20 ± 5 ℃, humidity ≤ 60% RH
- Diamond paste: about 5–8 g per square metre, used with a dedicated polishing cloth
- **Brushing machine** — constant-speed machine, adjustable 0.5–2 m/min
- **Brushing abrasive** — nylon wheel or belt, grit 1000#–2000#
- **Mirror abrasive** — wet sandpaper 3000#–5000#, diamond paste 8000#–15000#
- **Cleaning method** — ultrasonic plus DI-water rinse plus hot-air drying

> Key point: a mirror is not "polished" out — it is "ground" out. If the first six stages are not done thoroughly, everything after is wasted.

### 2.8 Step 08 — Polishing: bringing up the gloss

![Worker buffing a stainless panel to mirror finish with a cloth wheel](img:18)

- Cut with a sisal wheel and purple compound; finish with a cloth wheel and white compound — separate wheels, separate compounds, never mixed
- Keep moving at a constant speed; never dwell in one spot
- Keep the surface temperature under 150 ℃ to prevent oxidation yellowing
- Wipe immediately with a clean soft cloth after polishing to remove compound residue
- **Cutting stage** — sisal wheel plus purple compound removes fine-grinding marks at 2000–2500 rpm
- **Finishing stage** — cloth wheel plus white compound lifts the gloss at 2500–3000 rpm
- **Temperature** — monitor live with an IR thermometer; pause immediately if over temperature
- **Compound residue** — wash with a dedicated degreaser to avoid harming PVD adhesion

> Key point: polishing "brightens", it does not "correct". Overheating is the number-one cause of yellowed mirrors and orange peel.

### 2.9 Step 09 — Surface treatment: PVD coating and sealing

![PVD vacuum coating chamber with freshly coated gold and black titanium panels](img:19)

- PVD vacuum coating: gold, champagne, rose gold, black titanium, gunmetal, antique bronze — uniform colour, no difference
- Options include fluorocarbon spray, anodising and chemical antique-bronze ageing
- AF anti-fingerprint sealing cuts lifetime maintenance cost significantly
- Before PVD: no dust, no moisture, no oil residue
- **PVD temperature** — 150–250 ℃; the low-temperature process avoids distorting the base material
- **Vacuum level** — ≤ 5 × 10⁻³ Pa ensures a dense coating
- **Targets** — titanium, chromium and zirconium targets, selected by colour code
- **Seal thickness** — nano-scale 20–50 nm, no effect on feel or gloss

> Key point: before PVD the surface must be dust-free, moisture-free and oil-free — a single fingerprint becomes a permanent flaw.

### 2.10 Step 10 — Inspection, packing and installation

![Inspector measuring surface roughness on a mirror panel](img:20)

![Finished stainless steel panels packed in a custom wooden crate](img:21)

- Piece-by-piece testing: roughness, gloss, colour difference, adhesion (cross-hatch)
- Dust-free film → pearl cotton → custom wooden crate; stacking and hard contact strictly prohibited
- Dust-free installation on site, cotton gloves throughout, fingerprints wiped uniformly after installation
- Final sealing and acceptance completed within 24 hours of installation
- **Roughness tester** — Mitutoyo SJ-210 or equal accuracy; 3 points per piece, averaged
- **Gloss meter** — 60° incidence, 5 points per square metre
- **Colorimeter** — D65 light source, ΔE ≤ 1.5, ≤ 1.0 on the same face
- **Packing standard** — pearl cotton plus bubble wrap plus custom crate, corner protectors on all edges

> Key point: the last "grinding" before delivery is protection.

## 3. Process Data and Quantified Metrics

### 3.1 Abrasive grit vs. surface roughness

Each grit step lowers Ra by roughly 30–50%; the vertical axis is logarithmic.

- **Coarse stage** — 80# → 120#, Ra falls to about 3.2 μm
- **Medium-fine stage** — 240# → 400# → 600#, Ra falls to about 0.8 μm and then 0.2 μm
- **Fine stage** — 800# → 3000#, Ra converges towards 0.05 μm; 8000# diamond paste brings mirror parts to Ra ≤ 0.012 μm
- **Working rule** — never jump more than one grit class; a skipped stage leaves scratches that every later stage amplifies

### 3.2 Work-hour share by process step

Based on 1 ㎡ of mirror stainless component, seven main steps:

- Fine finishing (brushing and mirror): 20%
- Coarse grinding: 18%
- Medium grinding: 15%
- Polishing: 15%
- Fine grinding: 14%
- Surface treatment: 10%
- Inspection and packing: 8%
- **Conclusion** — grinding-type steps together take more than 80% of the hours; this is where schedules are won or lost

### 3.3 Gloss of common surface treatments

- 2B mill finish — 30 GU
- Frost sand / short grain — 80 GU
- Long-grain brushing — 180 GU
- Satin / matte — 250 GU
- 8K mirror — 800+ GU

### 3.4 Polishing temperature vs. time control

Surface temperature must stay strictly under 150 ℃ — over-temperature causes oxidation yellowing and orange peel.

- **Safe zone** — ≤ 150 ℃ throughout the pass
- **Danger line** — 150 ℃; live monitoring with an IR thermometer
- **Method** — constant movement, never dwelling in one spot; pause immediately when the surface approaches the line

## 4. Brushing Direction and Corner Treatment

Direction is the hidden rule behind a unified look: the same wall can look "patchwork" purely because grain directions disagree.

![Brushed grain on face A running at 45 degrees upward](img:22)

![Brushed grain on face B mirrored at 45 degrees downward](img:23)

- **One face, one direction** — all panels on the same face must run the grain in exactly the same direction; mixed directions create an obvious patchwork look over large areas
- **45° corner matching** — each side of an external corner takes 45°, meeting on a symmetric bisector; never butt them head-on or run the grain perpendicular to the corner
- **Grain straightness ≤ 1 mm/m** — hand brushing easily produces waviness; large areas must use constant-speed machine brushing
- **No broken grain, no overlap marks** — stopping or pulling back mid-pass causes both; one pass, constant speed, all the way
- **Consistent joint direction** — adjacent panels must run the same direction with the grain aligned at the joint, avoiding T-junction grain
- **Internal and external corners** — at internal corners the grain should die into the corner; at external corners it should open symmetrically — a visual extension, never a cut-off

## 6. Common Defects, Causes and Remedies

Twelve defects cover nearly every polish-related claim; each has a mechanical cause and a mechanical fix.

- **Orange peel (frequent)** — cause: polishing temperature too high, coarse base grains, skipping grit stages · remedy: keep ≤ 150 ℃, choose fine-grain base material, step through every grit
- **Colour difference (fatal)** — cause: sheets from different heat numbers mixed, PVD coated in separate batches · remedy: purchase by heat number, coat the whole batch in one furnace run, pre-lay panels to compare colour before delivery
- **Waviness** — cause: uneven hand-brushing speed and fluctuating pressure · remedy: switch to constant-speed machine brushing with fixed pressure and feed
- **Residual scratches** — cause: skipped grits, poor cleaning between steps, trapped abrasive grains · remedy: strictly apply the 90° cross method — change direction at every grit and clean thoroughly
- **Weld scars and hollows** — cause: excessive weld heat input, uneven coarse-grinding pressure · remedy: low-current fast welding, crossing passes and even pressure in coarse grinding
- **Fingerprints** — cause: no AF sealing, bare-handed installation · remedy: seal uniformly before delivery, cotton gloves throughout installation
- **Oxidation yellowing** — cause: polishing overheating, long storage in humid conditions · remedy: strict temperature control, store finished goods dry, seal promptly to exclude air
- **Sharp edges** — cause: faces treated but edge breaking ignored · remedy: complete the R ≥ 2 mm radiusing and edge breaking during medium grinding
- **PVD delamination** — cause: dirty pre-treatment, insufficient vacuum, coating too thick · remedy: ultrasonic cleaning plus DI-water rinse plus hot-air drying; vacuum ≤ 5 × 10⁻³ Pa
- **Joint misalignment** — cause: cumulative cutting tolerances, installation datums not unified · remedy: pre-assemble in the workshop, unify datum lines on site, tight joints ≤ 0.3 mm
- **Water ripple** — cause: poor base-material flatness, excessive polishing pressure · remedy: base material with flatness ≤ 1.0 mm/m, moderate even polishing pressure
- **Broken brush grain** — cause: mid-pass stops, uneven abrasive grit size · remedy: one pass at constant speed to the end, nylon wheels with uniform grit

## 8. Cost Structure and Schedule

### 8.1 Cost breakdown per square metre

All-in price roughly 1800–3200 CNY/㎡ depending on finish and batch size:

- Base material (304 / 316L): 38%
- Grinding and polishing labour: 26%
- PVD / surface treatment: 18%
- Equipment depreciation and energy: 8%
- Packing and transport: 6%
- Management and margin: 4%
- **Reading** — labour plus surface treatment approach half the cost, which is why process discipline directly protects margin

From sample sign-off to delivery, excluding on-site installation and care:

- D1–D3 — Sample approval: produce 300 × 300 mm panels; client signs and seals
- D4–D6 — Material purchase and inspection: purchase by heat number; check flatness and surface

## 9. Acceptance Standards and Test Methods

Every item below is testable with a named instrument and a written method — the same numbers should appear in the contract.

- **Surface roughness Ra** — requirement: mirror ≤ 0.012 μm, brushed ≤ 0.1 μm · instrument: roughness tester · method: 3 points per piece, averaged, points 50 mm clear of welds
- **Gloss GU** — requirement: mirror ≥ 800, brushed 150–250 · instrument: gloss meter (60°) · method: 5 points per square metre, averaged
- **Colour difference ΔE** — requirement: ≤ 1.5, ≤ 1.0 on the same face · instrument: colorimeter (D65) · method: compare with the sealed sample, 10% sampled per batch
- **PVD adhesion** — requirement: cross-hatch class 0 (no flaking) · instrument: cross-hatch cutter plus 3M tape · method: scribe a 1 mm × 1 mm grid, apply tape, tear off quickly
- **Film thickness** — requirement: 0.5–1.5 μm · instrument: thickness gauge · method: 3 points per piece — edge and centre
- **Flatness** — requirement: ≤ 1.0 mm/m · instrument: 2 m straightedge plus feeler gauge · method: fit the straightedge, measure the widest gap
- **Joint width** — requirement: tight ≤ 0.3 mm, invisible ≤ 0.5 mm · instrument: feeler gauge / caliper · method: measure along the full joint, record the maximum
- **Edge radius** — requirement: R ≥ 2 mm (R ≥ 3 mm in child areas) · instrument: radius gauge · method: 20% sampled, focusing on touchable edges
- **Anti-fingerprint effect** — requirement: contact angle ≥ 110° · instrument: contact-angle meter · method: 3 pieces per batch, 3 points each
- **Visual defects** — requirement: at 1 m, no orange peel, no scratches, no colour difference · instrument: strong torch plus loupe · method: 45° side light at 1 m distance, piece by piece

> The premium feel of metal never comes from the material itself — it is earned by every step, in microns over large areas. 80# → 8000#, ten steps, one forming pass.
