A material schedule for a commercial interior can include a display case, the framing behind it and several door or counter edges. These components may have different exposure, forming and maintenance requirements even when the surfaces appear to match. List the components by location and function, then choose the substrate, surface finish and any decorative coating for each. This makes competing bids easier to compare and shows where an alternative requires design approval.

## Screen 304 and 316L by exposure before pricing

Type 304 is a useful option to evaluate for many dry, regularly cleaned interior components. The 316 family adds molybdenum, which improves resistance to chloride attack compared with 304. The L in 316L denotes low carbon, a useful distinction when assessing corrosion at welded joints. The project specifier still needs to account for the environment and fabrication method when choosing a grade. [International Molybdenum Association stainless steel FAQ](https://www.imoa.info/molybdenum-uses/molybdenum-grade-stainless-steels/molybdenum-grade-stainless-steel-faq.php)

Start the exposure review by marking each part of the assembly. A sheltered vestibule near salt spray or deicing salts may collect deposits where rain never rinses the metal. Ask how the window line, joints and fasteners can be cleaned. Drainage, crevices, the finish and maintenance also affect performance. For severe or uncertain exposure, ask the project specifier to review suitable grades before accepting an alternate. [IMOA site and design evaluation guide](https://www.imoa.info/molybdenum-uses/molybdenum-grade-stainless-steels/architecture/stainless-steel-selection-system.php)

### Review welds and fixings as separate line items

Include the base metal and finish around exposed welds, concealed brackets and fasteners in the bid schedule. Ask how the supplier will handle weld heat tint, surface contamination and any required post-weld treatment. Specify the documentation needed to verify the delivered grade. A bracket photograph is useful for discussing the junction and access for finishing.

## Choose the copper alloy and expected appearance over time

Brass and bronze are families of copper alloys. In architectural usage, a product sold with a bronze appearance may be a copper alloy, a patinated surface or a finish applied to another metal. Ask bidders for the specific alloy designation and component form before comparing them. For a rail or hardware item, the approved alloy also needs to suit the forming, joining and service requirements. [Copper Development Association architectural handbook](https://www.copper.org/publications/pub_list/pdf/A4050-Architectural-Handbook.pdf)

A brass vitrine and a brass-toned stainless fixture can both suit a warm colour brief. The review should include what will happen at corners, handled surfaces and repair areas over time. If the design calls for brass to age visibly, agree an appearance range and maintenance routine. For a protected tone, review the clear coat, touch-up process and expected renewal with the supplier. [Copper Development Association architectural handbook](https://www.copper.org/publications/pub_list/pdf/A4050-Architectural-Handbook.pdf)

## Evaluate PVD as a coating option

Physical vapour deposition (PVD) can give stainless steel a bronze or brass tone while allowing the underlying texture to show. Ask the supplier to identify the base grade and finish, where the coating is applied in the sequence and the practical replacement plan for scratched faces. Where salt or pollution is a concern, assess the stainless grade independently. [IMOA guidance on coloured stainless steel](https://www.imoa.info/stainless-solutions/archive/18/colored-stainless-steel.php?m=1683978049)

## Check the bill of materials before awarding the package

1. Locate each visible facing, bracket, fastener and concealed frame by drawing reference.
2. List the requested grade or alloy designation and the thickness or section separately from the visual finish.
3. Identify salt exposure, wet crevices and dissimilar-metal interfaces for the specifier to assess.
4. Ask for material documentation and a formed finish sample with an exposed joint.
5. Require each alternate to describe where the metal, colour system, service assumptions or maintenance provisions change.
