Metalwork cutting and fabrication turns raw stainless steel, brass, copper and aluminium sheet into the finished surfaces seen in luxury boutiques, premium residences and five-star hotels. This deep-dive guide covers the complete chain: material grade selection, surface finishing, nesting and cutting, V-grooving and bending, welding and polishing, PVD coating, junction details, factory inspection and site acceptance. It is written as a standardised working reference for high-end metal finishing work such as 8K mirror, PVD titanium coating, snowflake brushing and antique-bronze ageing.

Headline control targets for the whole process:

- Core process nodes: 9
- Single-part dimensional tolerance: ±0.5 mm
- Joint (reveal) width control: ≤ 0.3 mm
- Sheet nesting utilisation: ≥ 92%
- Batch colour difference cap: ΔE ≤ 1.5

## 1. The Full Process at a Glance

The whole job runs as a ten-step closed loop across three stages: design and preparation (steps 01–03), cutting and forming (steps 04–06), and finishing and delivery (steps 07–10).

![Fibre laser cutters and CNC press brakes on the shop floor of a stainless steel fabrication workshop, with sheet stock racked alongside](img:01)

![Ten-step closed-loop cutting workflow divided into three stage zones, with a first-article confirmation loop from bending back to re-measurement](img:02)

- **Stage 1 — Design and preparation.** 01 Shop-drawing development (interior drawings + BIM modelling + junction details) → 02 On-site re-measurement (3D laser scanning + point-cloud verification) → 03 Material selection (sheet grade and surface finish sign-off)
- **Stage 2 — Cutting and forming.** 04 Nesting (nesting optimisation and cutting-program output) → 05 Cutting and blanking (fibre laser / waterjet / shearing) → 06 V-grooving and bending (forming and radius control)
- **Stage 3 — Finishing and delivery.** 07 Welding and polishing (laser welding + step-by-step polishing) → 08 Surface finishing (PVD / brushed / mirror) → 09 Inspection and packing (colour difference + protective film + timber crates) → 10 Transport and installation (sub-framing + dry-hanging + joint closure)
- **First-article loop.** A feedback line runs from 06 bending back to 02 re-measurement, forming a "first-article confirmation · dimensional verification" loop — the trial-bent first article is compared against site measurements here, and batch blanking may only start once it is approved.
- **How to read the diagram.** Solid gold arrows show forward process flow; the three stage zones correspond one-to-one with the actual operations, with no cross-stage shortcuts.

**Key controls:** uniform reveal depth away from the wall ｜ dimensional tolerance ±0.5 mm ｜ joints ≤ 0.3 mm ｜ nesting utilisation ≥ 92%

## 2. Materials and Surface Finishing

### 2.1 Common base-metal grades

Grades are ranked by a combined view of corrosion resistance, workability and cost; welding process and finish compatibility must be considered at the same time.

- **304 (06Cr19Ni10)** — Composition: Cr 18–20 / Ni 8–10.5 · Typical thickness: 0.8–3.0 mm · Corrosion resistance: good · Workability: bends and welds well, best value · Typical use: interior walls, ceilings, column cladding
- **304L (022Cr19Ni10)** — Composition: Cr 18–20 / Ni 9–13 / C ≤ 0.03 · Typical thickness: 1.0–6.0 mm · Corrosion resistance: good · Workability: low-carbon, no intergranular corrosion after welding · Typical use: thick-plate structural parts, welded assemblies
- **316L (022Cr17Ni12Mo2)** — Composition: Cr 16–18 / Ni 10–14 / Mo 2–3 · Typical thickness: 0.8–3.0 mm · Corrosion resistance: excellent · Workability: molybdenum-bearing, resists chloride attack · Typical use: luxury boutiques, coastal projects, bathrooms
- **201 (12Cr17Mn6Ni5N)** — Composition: Cr 16–18 / Mn 5.5–7.5 · Typical thickness: 0.5–2.0 mm · Corrosion resistance: fair · Workability: low cost, easy to form, weak corrosion resistance · Typical use: non-critical areas, budget projects
- **H62 brass** — Composition: Cu 62 / Zn 38 · Typical thickness: 0.8–3.0 mm · Corrosion resistance: moderate · Workability: oxidises and discolours easily, needs seal-glaze protection · Typical use: door casings, skirtings, decorative trims
- **H65 brass** — Composition: Cu 65 / Zn 35 · Typical thickness: 0.8–3.0 mm · Corrosion resistance: moderate · Workability: golden tone, good ductility · Typical use: premium hardware, decorative lines
- **T2 copper** — Composition: Cu ≥ 99.9 · Typical thickness: 1.0–4.0 mm · Corrosion resistance: good · Workability: excellent thermal and electrical conductivity, ages well · Typical use: feature walls, ceiling forms
- **5052 aluminium sheet** — Composition: Al–Mg alloy · Typical thickness: 1.0–6.0 mm · Corrosion resistance: good · Workability: lightweight, easy to bend, accepts PVDF coating · Typical use: large-format ceilings, curtain walls
- **6061 aluminium profile** — Composition: Al–Mg–Si alloy · Typical thickness: profile sections · Corrosion resistance: good · Workability: high strength, can be anodised · Typical use: sub-frames, edge trims, structural parts

### 2.2 Surface finishing processes

Finish codes follow GB/T 3280 and JIS G4305; the signed sample governs in practice.

- **2B** — Cold-rolled matte, grey-white · Roughness Ra 0.1–0.5 μm · Wear resistance: medium · Anti-fingerprint: no · Note: base finish, can be reworked
- **BA** — Bright annealed, uniform gloss · Roughness Ra 0.05–0.2 μm · Wear resistance: medium · Anti-fingerprint: no · Note: better than 2B, usable as a brushing base
- **8K mirror** — High-reflection mirror · Roughness Ra ≤ 0.01 μm · Wear resistance: low · Anti-fingerprint: optional · Note: protective film mandatory, no bare-hand contact
- **No.4 straight-line brushing** — Unidirectional linear grain · Roughness Ra 0.2–0.6 μm · Wear resistance: high · Anti-fingerprint: optional · Note: grain direction must be consistent
- **Snowflake / random brushing** — Short interwoven snowflake pattern · Roughness Ra 0.4–0.9 μm · Wear resistance: high · Anti-fingerprint: optional · Note: hides light scratches
- **SB shot blasting** — Uniform fine-blast matte · Roughness Ra 0.8–1.6 μm · Wear resistance: high · Anti-fingerprint: yes · Note: glass bead / aluminium oxide media
- **PVD titanium coating** — Gold / black titanium / rose gold · Roughness: follows base grain · Wear resistance: very high · Anti-fingerprint: optional · Note: film thickness 0.3–0.5 μm
- **Etching** — Recessed or raised patterns / lettering · Roughness: — · Wear resistance: medium · Anti-fingerprint: no · Note: requires film tooling
- **Antique bronze ageing** — Imitation bronze patina / deep brown · Roughness: follows base grain · Wear resistance: medium · Anti-fingerprint: yes · Note: needs seal-glaze protection
- **AFP anti-fingerprint** — Nano coating, resists fingerprints · Roughness: follows base grain · Wear resistance: high · Anti-fingerprint: yes · Note: first choice for luxury boutiques

### 2.3 Common PVD colour references

Plates in the same batch must be coated in the same furnace, with colour difference controlled to ΔE ≤ 1.5; different base textures (mirror / brushed / blasted) shift the perceived colour noticeably.

![PVD colour sample plates in titanium gold, black titanium, rose gold, champagne gold and antique bronze laid out on a dark stone surface](img:03)

![PVD colour card with five reference colours and their hex values](img:04)

- **Titanium gold (TiN), #C8A24A** — the classic champagne-gold tone; mostly used for door casings and decorative trims
- **Black titanium (TiCN), #3B3630** — low-reflection deep charcoal; suits large panels and tech-driven spaces
- **Rose gold, #C98A6B** — warm copper-pink; the signature colour of luxury boutiques
- **Champagne gold, #D9C48F** — the brightest light gold; suits ceilings and large bright surfaces
- **Antique bronze gold (ZrN), #A8915E** — a darker aged bronze; often paired with antique-bronze ageing
- **How colour renders** — codes are target colours only; actual appearance varies strongly with base texture (mirror / brushed / blasted), so sign-off must use samples with the same texture

**Controls:** film thickness 0.3–0.5 μm ｜ same batch must be same-furnace ｜ colour difference ΔE ≤ 1.5 ｜ adhesion: cross-hatch test with 3M tape

## 3. Working Points of the Nine Process Steps

### 3.1 Shop-drawing development and site re-measurement

- Take the architectural / interior / fine-finish detail drawings and build a dedicated metal-finish shop-drawing set
- Scan on site with Faro / RTC360 / Leica BLK 3D scanners; build point-cloud models and compare against design
- Re-measurement accuracy: ≤ 1 mm per part, cumulative long-edge error ≤ 3 mm
- Fix the closure relationships and reveal depths against stone, timber veneer, light coves and glazing
- Verify M&E points (switches, sockets, diffusers, sprinklers) against panel cut-out positions
- Outputs: fabrication drawings, cutting lists, hardware schedules, process cards, installation sequence drawings

### 3.2 Material selection and procurement

- Base materials: 304 / 316L stainless steel, H62 brass, copper, 5052 aluminium, titanium-gold clad sheet
- Finish thicknesses: 0.8 / 1.0 / 1.2 / 1.5 / 2.0 mm; structural parts 3–8 mm
- Surfaces: 8K mirror, snowflake / straight brushing, shot blasting, PVD titanium, antique bronze, etching
- Incoming inspection: colour-code comparison, grain-direction consistency, intact protective film, no scratches
- Luxury boutiques should prioritise anti-fingerprint (AFP) sheet
- All sheet for one project must come from the same batch and furnace number to avoid colour difference

### 3.3 Layout and nesting optimisation

- Software: SigmaNEST / Radan / AutoNest / Lantek automatic nesting
- Principles: consistent grain direction, bend lines clear of pattern joints, offcuts numbered and returned to stock
- Utilisation target ≥ 92%; mirror plate needs a 5–10 mm protective margin
- Output DXF / DWG cutting programs plus a part-number cross-reference table
- Oversized parts must consider transport and lift dimensions; segment and assemble on site if necessary
- The layout must be signed off by the project lead before blanking starts

### 3.4 Cutting and blanking

- Fibre laser: 1–4 mm stainless steel, accuracy ±0.1 mm, nitrogen cutting avoids oxidation
- Waterjet: first choice for mirror plate and copper; no thermal distortion, no heat-affected zone
- Shearing / plasma: thick plate and long straight cuts
- Apply protective film before cutting; deburr and soften edges immediately after
- Shaped parts (curves, piercings, etching) need a 1:1 template approved first
- Mirror plate needs double protective film during laser cutting to block spatter

### 3.5 V-grooving and bending

- Groove depth = sheet thickness × 0.5–0.6 (a 1.2 mm sheet is grooved 0.7 mm)
- Bend radius to design; springback compensation is required
- Angle tolerance ±0.5°; no orange peel or cracking allowed after bending
- Mirror-plate bend direction must avoid the primary sight line
- Anneal thick plate before bending to prevent stress cracking
- Check straightness after bending with a straightedge and feeler gauges

### 3.6 Welding and polishing

- Processes: laser welding (first choice for thin sheet) / TIG welding (304 with ER308L filler)
- Degrease before welding; deslag immediately after to stop oxide colour soaking in
- Polishing sequence: 120# → 240# → 400# → 800# → buffing
- Weld zones need same-colour treatment to avoid a "black line"
- Mirror-plate welds need 100% visual plus side-light inspection
- Welding current: 30–60 A for thin sheet to avoid burn-through

### 3.7 Surface finishing

- Brushing: 180# / 240# / 320#; grain direction must match the drawings
- Mirror: 8K polish, roughness Ra ≤ 0.01 μm
- PVD titanium: titanium gold / black titanium / rose gold / champagne gold, film 0.3–0.5 μm
- Ageing: potassium sulphide / bronze solution, then seal-glaze protection
- Same batch, same furnace run, colour difference controlled to ΔE ≤ 1.5
- Before PVD: ultrasonic cleaning plus ion bombardment to guarantee film adhesion

### 3.8 Inspection and packing

- Gauges: vernier calipers, squares, feeler gauges, colorimeter, film-thickness gauge, roughness tester
- 100% checks: dimensions, diagonals, angles, flatness, colour difference, surface defects
- Protection: mirror plate gets anti-static film + pearl wool + custom timber crates
- Parts numbered by installation position inside the crate, with packing list and installation drawings
- Photograph everything before transport as the basis for arrival acceptance
- Add desiccant inside crates; sea-freight jobs need vacuum packing

### 3.9 Transport and site installation

- Sub-frames: galvanised square tube / aluminium framing, with anti-corrosion and anti-galvanic treatment
- Fixing: dry-hanging clamps / under-cut anchors / structural adhesive; never fix mirror plate directly with self-tapping screws
- Joint control ≤ 0.3 mm, with uniform gap widths
- Sealant: neutral silicone, colour matched to the panel
- After installation: peel film, clean, protect the finished work, and re-inspect before handover
- Dissimilar-metal contact faces need isolating washers to prevent galvanic corrosion

## 4. Typical Junction Details

### 4.1 Node A — Dry-hung wall panel (horizontal section)

Used on concrete / blockwork walls; the metal panel is fixed to vertical sub-framing through aluminium hanging brackets.

![Brushed stainless steel wall cladding with fine even panel joints in a luxury hotel corridor](img:05)

![Node A section drawing: wall substrate, M8 chemical anchors, galvanised sub-frame, aluminium hanging bracket and folded panel edge](img:06)

- **Build-up** — wall substrate → M8 chemical anchors → 40×40×2.0 galvanised square-tube framing → aluminium hanging bracket → 1.2–2.0 mm metal panel
- **Reveal depth** — set by the framing and bracket thickness, typically 30–60 mm; it must be uniform across one visual plane to avoid a stepped look
- **Joint control** — folded panel edges butt against neighbouring panels at ≤ 0.3 mm; the joint opening should run with the sight line
- **Fixing** — dry-hanging is adjustable; brackets allow ±5 mm fine adjustment in three directions to absorb structural and framing tolerances
- **Do not** — never fix mirror plate directly with self-tapping screws; add isolating washers between dissimilar metals to prevent galvanic corrosion

**Units: mm ｜ Applies to: concrete / blockwork walls ｜ References: JGJ 133, GB/T 3280 ｜ Bracket capacity ≥ 3× self-weight**

### 4.2 Node B — Metal panel meeting stone flush

Both finishes sit on the same finished plane with a 3–5 mm movement joint sealed in colour-matched weatherproof silicone.

![Champagne-gold metal trim meeting polished marble perfectly flush in a luxury interior](img:07)

![Node B section drawing: stone and metal panel on one finished plane with a 3–5 mm movement joint and a 20 mm folded edge](img:08)

- **Finished plane** — stone and metal share one finished plane; the green dashed line in the drawing is the plane control line
- **Movement joint** — a 3–5 mm joint is kept between the two, sealed with neutral silicone weatherproof sealant colour-matched to the panels
- **Folded edge** — the panel edge is folded 20 mm, which stiffens the panel and hides the substrate and fixing zone
- **Why the joint is mandatory** — stone and metal have quite different thermal expansion coefficients; a hard, tight butt joint will buckle under thermal movement, so the joint releases that movement

**Units: mm ｜ Joint width: 3–5 mm ｜ Sealant: neutral silicone weatherproof ｜ Fold: 20 mm**

### 4.3 Node C — Ceiling shadow gap and light cove

Panel edges are folded; a concealed light cove sits behind, and the gap doubles as a decorative line.

![Metal ceiling panels with a recessed linear light cove and warm hidden LED lighting](img:09)

![Node C section drawing: structural slab, hangers, light-gauge or aluminium framing, folded panel edges, shadow gap and concealed light cove](img:10)

- **Build-up** — structural slab → hangers → light-gauge steel / aluminium framing → metal panel (edges folded for closure)
- **Shadow gap** — panels are set 20–30 mm apart; the opening works both as a decorative line and as the light outlet of the cove
- **Cove detail** — a concealed cove hangs inside the gap, about 60 mm clear, with an 8 W/m LED strip behind a diffuser to avoid visible diode spotting
- **Accuracy** — finished-plane flatness ≤ 1.0 mm / 2 m; hanger and framing spacing ≤ 1200 mm to prevent panel sag

**Units: mm ｜ Gap: 20–30 mm ｜ Flatness ≤ 1.0 mm / 2 m**

### 4.4 Node D — Column cladding internal corner

Column corners use a single folded plate or a 45° mitre, keeping cross welds away from the visual focal point.

![Mirror-polished stainless steel column cladding with a crisp internal corner in a luxury retail store](img:11)

![Node D section drawing: column cladding with folded radius corner, 45° mitre option and sealed internal corner](img:12)

- **Cladding method** — fold the side plate as one piece with a radius corner, or mitre at 45°, so cross welds never land on the visual focal point
- **Radius control** — bend radius R ≥ 1.5t (R2 for a 1.2 mm sheet): enough to avoid cracking and easy to clean; exposed external corners prefer one-piece folding
- **Internal corner closure** — tight butt joint plus colour-matched sealant; wide sealant beads are not allowed; the mitre in the drawing is only for cases where one-piece folding is impossible
- **Sequence** — bend first, then weld; keep welds off the column's frontal sight line and polish step by step to a matched colour

**Units: mm ｜ Bend radius R ≥ 1.5t ｜ Joints ≤ 0.3 mm ｜ External corners: one-piece folding preferred**

## 5. Nesting, Layout and Cutting Lists

### 5.1 Smart nesting layout (one 2440 × 1220 sheet)

Parts are arranged in a single grain direction; offcut strips at the bottom are numbered and returned to stock for later small parts.

![Fibre laser cutting head cutting nested parts out of a full stainless steel sheet, sparks and cutting fumes visible](img:13)

![Nesting diagram for a 2440 × 1220 sheet with eight parts in two rows, numbered offcut strips and the grain-direction arrow](img:14)

- **Sheet size** — standard sheet 2440 × 1220 mm; the layout shows 4 parts in row A and 4 in row B, 8 parts in total
- **Layout principle** — all parts run in one grain direction (the arrow at bottom right); bend lines must clear pattern joints
- **Offcut management** — offcut strips along the bottom are numbered into stock with size and material recorded, ready for secondary use on small parts
- **Protective margins** — mirror plate keeps an extra 5–10 mm border on all sides so cutting spatter and handling knocks never reach the usable area

**Units: mm ｜ Sheet 2440 × 1220 ｜ Utilisation 93.4% ｜ 8 parts ｜ 2 offcut strips**

### 5.2 V-grooving → bending

V-groove depth is held at 50–60% of sheet thickness; the bend radius and angle tolerance directly drive joint quality and flatness.

![CNC press brake bending a 1.2 mm brushed stainless steel sheet with a worker aligning the panel against the die](img:15)

![V-grooving and bending diagram: groove depth at 0.5–0.6 of thickness, angle tolerance ±0.5°, radius R ≥ 1.5t](img:16)

- **Grooving** — V-groove depth = thickness × 0.5–0.6; at t = 1.2 mm the groove is about 0.7 mm deep, leaving a 0.5 mm web so the bend does not crack
- **Bending** — angle tolerance ±0.5°, with springback compensation set per material and die; bend radius R ≥ 1.5t
- **Inspection** — check straightness after bending with a straightedge and feeler gauges; mirror-plate bend direction must avoid the main sight line to prevent a bright line
- **Common defects** — too deep and the bend cracks, too shallow and the angle falls short; worn tooling causes batch angle drift and needs regular calibration

**Units: mm ｜ Groove depth 0.5–0.6t ｜ Angle tolerance ±0.5° ｜ Bend radius R ≥ 1.5t**

## 7. Common Quality Defects and Countermeasures

- **Out-of-tolerance dimensions** — stage: cutting / bending · causes: inaccurate re-measurement, program errors, uncompensated bending springback · prevention: on-site 3D scanning + first-article confirmation + springback compensation table · rework: medium
- **Surface scratches** — stage: whole process · causes: handling knocks, dirty worktables, film peeled too early · prevention: film throughout + soft bench pads + peel film last · rework: high
- **Visible colour difference** — stage: surface finishing · causes: different batches or furnace runs, inconsistent base texture · prevention: same-batch purchasing + same-furnace processing + 100% colorimeter checks · rework: high
- **Weld traces (black line)** — stage: welding / polishing · causes: weld oxidation, insufficient polishing, uneven buffing · prevention: laser welding + step polishing + same-colour treatment · rework: high
- **Bend angle deviation** — stage: bending · causes: wrong groove depth, worn tooling, springback · prevention: regular tooling calibration + trial-bent first article + 100% protractor checks · rework: medium
- **Wavy panel distortion** — stage: welding / transport · causes: excessive weld heat input, compression in transport · prevention: controlled welding current + crate supports + no stacking pressure · rework: high
- **PVD film peeling** — stage: surface finishing · causes: poor cleaning, insufficient adhesion · prevention: ultrasonic cleaning + ion bombardment + cross-hatch testing · rework: high
- **Uneven joints** — stage: installation · causes: uneven framing, insufficient bracket adjustment range · prevention: levelled framing + adjustable brackets + per-panel re-measurement · rework: medium
- **Galvanic corrosion** — stage: installation · causes: dissimilar metals in direct contact in damp conditions · prevention: isolating washers + anti-corrosion coating + same-alloy-family selection · rework: medium
- **Sealant yellowing / cracking** — stage: installation · causes: poor sealant quality, dirty substrate, excessive movement · prevention: neutral weatherproof sealant + cleaned substrate + movement joints · rework: medium

## 8. Process Differences Across the Three Business Types

- **Typical materials** — Luxury retail: 316L mirror stainless, brass, PVD titanium panels · Premium residential: 304 brushed stainless, brass, aluminium panels · Hotels: 316L stainless, copper, aluminium, titanium-gold clad sheet
- **Typical thickness** — Luxury retail: 1.2–2.0 mm · Residential: 0.8–1.5 mm · Hotels: 1.0–3.0 mm
- **Surface requirements** — Luxury retail: 8K mirror, anti-fingerprint, seamless, no colour difference · Residential: mainly brushed / matte, with emphasis on stain resistance · Hotels: mirror and brushed mixed, large-panel flatness first
- **Part characteristics** — Luxury retail: many small and shaped parts, curves and piercings · Residential: medium sizes, highly standardised · Hotels: large panels, extra-long parts, full-height column and ceiling wraps
- **Dimensional tolerance** — Luxury retail: ±0.5 mm · Residential: ±1.0 mm · Hotels: ±1.0 mm
- **Joint control** — Luxury retail: ≤ 0.3 mm, visually seamless · Residential: ≤ 1.0 mm · Hotels: ≤ 0.5 mm
- **Fabrication lead time** — Luxury retail: 15–25 days · Residential: 10–20 days · Hotels: 25–45 days
- **Key control points** — Luxury retail: no colour difference, no scratches, invisible joints · Residential: cost control and stable delivery · Hotels: large-panel flatness, transport distortion control
- **Recommended finish combinations** — Luxury retail: 8K mirror + AFP / PVD titanium brushed · Residential: straight brushing / snowflake + 2B base · Hotels: brushed + mirror mixes + PVD black titanium
